House rules

Irreverent about arguments. Scrupulous about what they say.

  1. State it fairly first. Every rebuke opens with the argument in a form its author would sign. Shredding a strawman is no fun and proves nothing.
  2. Attack the argument, not the person. The argument may be called dumb. Its author, who is generally cleverer than the argument, may not.
  3. Deal with the best reply to the reply. Famous arguments have been attacked before and their defenders have answered. A rebuke that ignores the known counter-moves isn't finished.
  4. Say what survives. Most bad arguments are wrapped around a real question. Each rebuke ends by saying what the defendant got right.
  5. Cite. Primary sources, with enough detail to find them.
  6. Right of reply. If a rebuke is itself a dumb argument, open an issue. Convictions can be overturned on appeal.
