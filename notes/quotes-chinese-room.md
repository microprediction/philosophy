# Other people's rebukes of the Chinese Room (internal notes)

Collected 2026-09-19 by a research agent. Wording was checked against downloaded sources unless marked.
The 1980 commentaries are in Behavioral and Brain Sciences 3(3), printed with Searle's paper:
https://home.csulb.edu/~cwallis/382/readings/482/searle.minds.brains.programs.bbs.1980.pdf

Check punctuation of OCR-sourced book quotes (Pinker, Hofstadter, Mind's I, Churchlands) against print before publishing.

1. Hofstadter, "Reductionism and religion", BBS 1980, p. 433.
   "This religious diatribe against AI, masquerading as a serious scientific argument, is one of the wrongest, most infuriating articles I have ever read in my life. It is matched in its power to annoy only by the famous article 'Minds, Machines, and Gödel' by J. R. Lucas (1961)."
   Also: "'intentionality' - his name for soul."

2. Dennett, NYRB exchange, 21 Dec 1995. https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1995/12/21/the-mystery-of-consciousness-an-exchange/
   "For his part, he has one argument, the Chinese Room, and he has been trotting it out, basically unchanged, for fifteen years. It has proven to be an amazingly popular number among the non-experts, in spite of the fact that just about everyone who knows anything about the field dismissed it long ago. It is full of well-concealed fallacies. By Searle's own count, there are over a hundred published attacks on it. He can count them, but I guess he can't read them..."
   "Why didn't I repeat that 1987 article in my 1991 book? Because, unlike Searle, I had gone on to other things."

3. Pat Hayes, reported first-hand by Harnad (2001). https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~harnad/Papers/Harnad/harnad00.searle.html
   "Pat Hayes went so far as to define cognitive science as 'the ongoing research program of showing Searle's Chinese Room Argument to be false' -- 'and silly,' I believe he added at the time"
   Harnad: "the overwhelming majority still think the Chinese Room Argument is dead wrong, even among those who agree that computers can't understand!"

4. Pinker, How the Mind Works (1997), pp. 94-95.
   "Searle's tactic is to appeal over and over to our common sense. You can almost hear him saying, 'Aw, c'mon! You mean to claim that the guy understands Chinese??!!! Geddadahere! He doesn't understand a word!! He's lived in Brooklyn all his life!!' and so on. But the history of science has not been kind to the simple intuitions of common sense, to put it mildly."
   "More than a hundred published articles have replied to it, and I have found it an excellent reason to take my name off all Internet discussion-group lists."
   "My own view is that Searle is merely exploring facts about the English word understand."
   "If a scientist explains the functioning of the human elbow by saying it is a second-class lever, it is no refutation to describe a guy holding a second-class lever made of steel and proclaim, 'But look, the guy doesn't have three elbows!!!'"

5. Dennett, "The milk of human intentionality", BBS 1980, pp. 428-430.
   "I want to distinguish Searle's arguments, which I consider sophistry, from his positive view..."
   "But nothing could be only the implementation of a formal program; computers exude heat and noise in the course of their operations - why not intentionality too?"
   "Luckily for us, though, our brains make intentionality; if they didn't, we'd behave just as we now do, but of course we wouldn't mean it!"

6. Minsky, "Decentralized minds", BBS 1980, p. 440.
   "I just can't see why Searle is so opposed to the idea that a really big pile of junk might have feelings like ours."

7. Rorty, "Searle and the special powers of the brain", BBS 1980, pp. 445-446.
   "Searle sets up the issues as would a fundamentalist Catholic defending transubstantiation."
   "How does Searle know that? In the same way, presumably, as the orthodox theologian knows things."
   "Perhaps, however, the money spent playing Turing games with expensive computers should have been used to subsidize relatively cheap philosophers like Searle and me."

8. Aaronson, Quantum Computing Since Democritus, lecture 4. https://www.scottaaronson.com/democritus/lec4.html
   "There has, on the other hand, been a non-insight, which is called Searle's Chinese Room."
   "Duh: you might not understand Chinese, but the rule book does!"
   "...much like the doofus freshmen who write (a+b)^2=a^2+b^2 on their math tests."
   On Searle and Penrose: "The only problem with these arguments is that they're also arguments against the possibility of thinking brains!"

9. Hofstadter, I Am a Strange Loop (2007), pp. 28-30.
   "In a ploy that, in my opinion, should fool only third-graders but that unfortunately takes in great multitudes of his professional colleagues..."

10. Pylyshyn, "The 'causal power' of machines", BBS 1980, p. 442.
   "Is Searle then proposing that intentionality is a substance secreted by the brain, and that a possible test for intentionality might involve, say, titrating the brain tissue that realized some putative mental episodes?"
   "...you would in all likelihood just keep right on speaking exactly as you are doing now except that you would eventually stop meaning anything by it."

11. Churchlands, "Could a Machine Think?", Scientific American, Jan 1990 (luminous room).
   https://patriciachurchland.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/1990-Could-A-Machine-Think.pdf
   "Rather than exploit one's understanding of these things, Searle's argument freely exploits one's ignorance of them."

12. Kurzweil, "Locked in His Chinese Room" (2002). Supports the scale argument.
   "A key to the philosophical sleight of hand implicit in the Chinese Room Argument has specifically to do with the complexity and scale of the system."

13. Hofstadter and Dennett, The Mind's I (1981), pp. 373-375. On memorising the program:
   "As if a human being could, by any conceivable stretch of the imagination, do this."
   "Searle - as deft at distracting the reader's attention as a practiced magician - switches the reader's image to a hypothetical program that passes the Turing test!"

14. Dennett, Intuition Pumps (2013), p. 320.
   "...it persuades by clouding our imagination, not exploiting it well."
   Relayed by Hofstadter (original not located): "It ain't the meat, it's the motion."

15. Fodor, BBS 1980, note 2: "If that's dualism, I imagine we can live with it."

16. Terry Bisson, "They're Made Out of Meat" (1990), linked to Searle by Pinker pp. 95-96.
   https://www.terrybisson.com/theyre-made-out-of-meat-2/
