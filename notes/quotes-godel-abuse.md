# Abuses of Gödel's theorem: sources (internal notes)

Collected 2026-09-19 by a research agent. VERIFIED = exact wording found in fetched text. SECONDARY = seen only as quoted by another author. SNIPPET = Google Books search-inside only (verbatim OCR, often truncated); check against print.
Sokal and Bricmont page numbers are from the 2011 Profile reprint of Intellectual Impostures. The US edition (Fashionable Nonsense) paginates differently.

## Flags
- "Either mathematics is too big for the human mind, or the human mind is more than a machine" is NOT a Gödel quote. It is a paraphrase (Goldblatt, Topoi, 1979). Use the Gibbs Lecture wording.
- Perry Marshall puts "Anything you can draw a circle around..." in quotation marks as what the theorem "says". It is his own sentence.
- Jordan Peterson tweet ("Proof itself, of any sort, is impossible, without an axiom (as Gödel proved). Thus faith in God is a prerequisite for all proof.") rests on Wikiquote only, dated 25 Nov 2013. Not verified at source. Not used on the site.
- Gödel and the Constitution: Morgenstern's 1971 memo says "inner contradictions" and never says what they were. Versions with dialogue are reconstructions. Nothing connects it to incompleteness.
- Feferman's 2006 lecture misdates the Gibbs Lecture (it was 1951) and loosely quotes his own NYRB letter. Use the letter.
- Lyotard only alludes to Gödel in passing; Badiou's example in S&B is the continuum hypothesis, not incompleteness. Neither is a headline abuser.
- Not retrieved: Bouveresse verbatim, law review quotes, Peter Smith, Hofstadter complaints, Rucker's mysticism chapter, Lennox/Craig/Poythress.

## Spare material not on the page
Shalizi: "A much-abused result in mathematical logic, supposed by many authors who don't understand it to support their own favored brand of rubbish". Close: "it doesn't shake the foundations of the house of intellect, or exalt us above all else that greps." http://bactra.org/notebooks/godels-theorem.html
Aaronson lec. 3: "the only mathematical theories pompous enough to prove their own consistency, are the ones that don't have any consistency to brag about!" https://www.scottaaronson.com/democritus/lec3.html
Franzén via Feferman: "nothing in the incompleteness theorem excludes the possibility of our producing a complete theory of stars, ghosts and cats, all rolled into one, as long as what we say about stars, ghosts and cats can't be interpreted as statements about the natural numbers."
Franzén, Notices p. 440: "even the most determined wet blanket cannot prevent people from appealing to the incompleteness theorem in contexts where its relevance is at best a matter of analogy or metaphor."
Raatikainen 2005: "Perhaps a person who is inclined to see evidence for God's existence everywhere can also see it in Gödel's theorems, but in themselves, these results have no such implications."
Paul Davies, The Mind of God (via Raatikainen): "Possibly the mystical path is a way to such understanding."
Guillen, Bridges to Infinity (via Raatikainen): "the only possible way of avowing an unprovable truth, mathematical or otherwise, is to accept it as an article of faith."
Bibliography of Christianity and Mathematics 1983 (via Feferman): "theologians can be comforted in their failure to systematize revealed truth because mathematicians cannot grasp all mathematical truths in their systems either."
George Gilder, Knowledge and Power (via Wikiquote): "Gödel demonstrated that every logical scheme, including mathematics, is dependent upon axioms that it cannot prove".
Ian Stewart slip (via Franzén): "Goedel showed that...if anyone finds a proof that arithmetic is consistent, then it isn't!"
Franzén's anonymous Usenet examples p. 77 (SNIPPET): the Bible "is a complete system, so Gödel seems to indicate it cannot be true"; "The U.S. Constitution is a formal system, after a fashion."
Morgenstern memo, 13 Sept 1971 (agent's OCR): "he had found some inner contradictions and that he could show in a perfectly legal manner it would be possible for somebody to become a dictator".
