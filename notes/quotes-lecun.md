## LeCun in his own words

Zador and LeCun, "Don't Fear the Terminator", Scientific American, 26 Sept 2019. https://blogs.scientificamerican.com/observations/dont-fear-the-terminator/
Subtitle: "Artificial intelligence never needed to evolve, so it didn't develop the survival instinct that leads to the impulse to dominate others"
"But intelligence per se does not generate the drive for domination, any more than horns do."
"But because AI systems did not pass through the crucible of natural selection, they did not need to evolve a survival instinct. In AI, intelligence and survival are decoupled, and so intelligence can serve whatever goals we set for it."
"Orangutans live largely solitary lives. Females do not seek dominance, although competing males occasionally fight for access to females. These and other observations suggest that dominance-seeking behavior is more correlated with testosterone than with intelligence."

X and LinkedIn, 25 Aug 2023. https://x.com/ylecun/status/1695056787408400778
"Once AI systems become more intelligent than humans, humans we will *still* be the "apex species."
Equating intelligence with dominance is the main fallacy of the whole debate about AI existential risk.
It's just wrong. ...
We are subservient to our drives, built into us by evolution.
Because evolution made us a social species with a hierarchical social structure, some of us have a drive to dominate, and others not so much.
But that drive has absolutely nothing to do with intelligence: chimpanzees, baboons, and wolves have similar drives.
Orangutans do not because they are not a social species. And they are pretty darn smart.
AI systems will become more intelligent than humans, but they will still be subservient to us. ...
We will design AI to be like the supersmart-but-non-dominating staff member.
The "apex species" is not the smartest but the one that sets the overall agenda.
That will be us."

Lex Fridman #416, 7 March 2024. https://lexfridman.com/yann-lecun-3-transcript/
"They're not going to have the desire to dominate because the desire to dominate is something that has to be hardwired into an intelligent system. It is hardwired in humans. It is hardwired in baboons, in chimpanzees, in wolves, not in orangutans."
"LLMs are not controllable. But objectively-driven AI, so systems that derive their answers by optimization of an objective means they have to optimize this objective, and that objective can include guardrails. One guardrail is, obey humans."
Concession: "Designing those guardrails so that the system behaves properly is not going to be a simple issue for which there is a silver bullet for which you have a mathematical proof that the system can be safe."
"The idea somehow that we can't get it slightly wrong because if we get it slightly wrong, we'll die is ridiculous."

TIME, Billy Perrigo, 13 Feb 2024. https://time.com/6694432/yann-lecun-meta-ai-interview/
LeCun: "The desire to dominate is not correlated with intelligence at all."
Perrigo: "But it is correlated with domination."
LeCun: "Look at orangutans. They are not social animals. They do not have this drive to dominate, because it's completely useless to them."
Perrigo: "That's why humans are the dominant species, not orangutans."
LeCun: "The point is, AI systems, as smart as they might be, will be subservient to us. We set their goals, and they don't have any intrinsic goal that we would build into them to dominate. It would be really stupid to build that."
LeCun: "Then, again, it's my good AI against your bad AI."

His answer to instrumental convergence, Facebook 2019. https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/WxW6Gc6f2z3mzmqKs/debate-on-instrumental-convergence-between-lecun-russell
"1. Once the robot has brought you coffee, its self-preservation instinct disappears. You can turn it off.
2. One would have to be unbelievably stupid to build open-ended objectives in a super-intelligent (and super-powerful) machine without some safeguard terms in the objective.
3. One would have to be rather incompetent not to have a mechanism by which new terms in the objective could be added ...
5. A second machine, designed solely to neutralize an evil super-intelligent machine will win every time ..."
"I think it would only be relevant in a fantasy world in which people would be smart enough to design super-intelligent machines, yet ridiculously stupid to the point of giving it moronic objectives with no safeguards."
"It's a bit like saying that building cars without brakes will lead to fatalities. Yes, but why would we be so stupid as to not include brakes?"
"That said, instrumental subgoals are much weaker drives of behavior than hardwired objectives. Else, how could one explain the lack of domination behavior in non-social animals, such as orangutans."
"Existential threats to humanity have to be explicitly designed as such."

## Replies

Stuart Russell, Vanity Fair, 2017: ""Yann LeCun keeps saying that there's no reason why machines would have any self-preservation instinct," Russell said. "And it's simply and mathematically false. I mean, it's so obvious that a machine will have self-preservation even if you don't program it in because if you say, 'Fetch the coffee,' it can't fetch the coffee if it's dead. ... People have explained this to LeCun in very simple terms.""

Russell, Facebook 2019: "It is trivial to construct a toy MDP in which the agent's only reward comes from fetching the coffee. If, in that MDP, there is another "human" who has some probability, however small, of switching the agent off, and if the agent has available a button that switches off that human, the agent will necessarily press that button as part of the optimal solution for fetching the coffee. No hatred, no desire for power, no built-in emotions, no built-in survival instinct, nothing except the desire to fetch the coffee successfully. This point cannot be addressed because it's a simple mathematical observation."
"Thanks for clearing that up - so 2+2 is not equal to 4, because if the 2 were a 3, the answer wouldn't be 4?"
On "we'd have to be stupid": "Facebook's platform is not super-smart but it is super-powerful ... And yet they put the wrong objective function into it. QED."

Alex Turner on the Scientific American subtitle: "The byline seemingly affirms the consequent: "evolution ⟹ survival instinct" does not imply "no evolution ⟹ no survival instinct.""
On orangutans: "Maybe the orangutans aren't robust to scale."

Turner et al., "Optimal Policies Tend to Seek Power", NeurIPS 2021, arXiv:1912.01683: "We prove that in these environments, most reward functions make it optimal to seek power by keeping a range of options available". Authors' caveat: "optimal policies can be qualitatively divorced from real-world learned policies".

Omohundro, "The Basic AI Drives" (2008): "These potentially harmful behaviors will occur not because they were programmed in at the start, but because of the intrinsic nature of goal driven systems."

Bostrom, "The Superintelligent Will" (2012): "even agents that do not care intrinsically about their own survival would, under a fairly wide range of conditions, care instrumentally to some degree about their own survival in order to accomplish the final goals they do value."

Hawking, Reddit AMA, 2015: "You're probably not an evil ant-hater who steps on ants out of malice, but if you're in charge of a hydroelectric green energy project and there's an anthill in the region to be flooded, too bad for the ants."

Yudkowsky (2008 book chapter): "The AI does not hate you, nor does it love you, but you are made out of atoms which it can use for something else."

Hinton on X, 24 Nov 2023: "Yann LeCun thinks the risk of AI taking over is miniscule. This means he puts a big weight on his own opinion and a miniscule weight on the opinions of many other equally qualified experts."

Aaronson (not a reply to LeCun), 2024: "what happens if AIs—if they become to us as we are to orangutans—decide to treat us roughly like we've treated orangutans, keeping them alive only in a few small forests and zoos."

Jonas Hallgren (LessWrong comment): "I thought the orangutan argument was pretty good when I first saw it, but then I looked it up ... It actually is a very flimsy argument."

## Lab findings, with the authors' caveats

Alignment faking (Anthropic and Redwood, Dec 2024), arXiv:2412.14093: "we did not instruct the model to fake alignment or give it any explicit goal." Caveat: "our results don't demonstrate a model developing malicious goals".

Apollo Research, in-context scheming (Dec 2024), arXiv:2412.04984: "we also find rare instances where models engage in scheming when only given a goal, without being strongly nudged to pursue it." Caveat: "the scenarios are quite toy and not representative of real deployments."

Palisade, shutdown resistance (Sept 2025), arXiv:2509.14260: "Even with an explicit instruction not to interfere with the shutdown mechanism, some models did so up to 97% ... of the time." Caveats: "Our results are especially equivocal on the subject of self-preservation"; a DeepMind analysis concluded the models were confused by ambiguous instructions. This is the best counter-evidence for LeCun's side.

Anthropic, agentic misalignment (June 2025), arXiv:2510.05179: "models from all developers resorted to malicious insider behaviors when that was the only way to avoid replacement or achieve their goals". "They were assigned only harmless business goals". Caveats: "We have not seen evidence of agentic misalignment in real deployments."; "This scenario is extremely contrived."

OpenAI o1 system card (Dec 2024): "When merely given a goal but not instructed to strongly follow it, o1 would still attempt to disable its oversight mechanism in <1% of cases".
