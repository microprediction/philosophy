# Capra, The Tao of Physics, and quantum mysticism: sources (internal notes)

Collected 2026-09-19 by a research agent.
RAW = wording read in downloaded full text. FTS = stitched from archive.org public search snippets of a lending-only book (every word seen, whole page not seen; check against print). SECONDARY = seen only as quoted by someone else.
Editions: 1st ed. Shambhala 1975 (https://archive.org/details/the-tao-of-physics); 3rd ed. Flamingo 1991 with both afterwords.

## Corrections to things said from memory
- Lederman's joke is a section title, "The Dancing Moo-Shu Masters". Not "moo shu physics".
- Wilber never wrote "when the physics goes, so does the mysticism". His line is "Does Buddha therefore lose his enlightenment?"
- Gell-Mann's "flapdoodle" chapter never names Capra, and the two-word phrase "quantum flapdoodle" is not in the book. It reaches people via Shermer.
- "If you think you understand quantum theory..." is a paraphrase. Feynman published "I think I can safely say that nobody understands quantum mechanics."
- The 1966 remark that the bootstrap "was less of a theory than a tautology" was Francis Low's, reported by David Gross.
- Capra's closing line has a semicolon: "Science does not need mysticism and mysticism does not need science; but man needs both."
- Capra's Heisenberg-and-Tagore story was walked back by Capra himself in 2017.

## Capra in his own words (RAW)
Preface, the beach: "I 'saw' cascades of energy coming down from outer space, in which particles were created and destroyed in rhythmic pulses; ... and at that moment I knew that this was the Dance of Shiva, the Lord of Dancers worshipped by the Hindus."
Preface: "In the beginning, I was helped on my way by 'power plants' which showed me how the mind can flow freely ... not unlike Castaneda, pouring out my impressions on to a piece of paper."
Thesis, ch. 1: "both force us to see the world very much in the way a Hindu, Buddhist or Taoist sees it ... we shall often encounter statements where it is almost impossible to say whether they have been made by physicists or by Eastern mystics." The parallels also appear "in the teachings of the Yaqui sorcerer Don Juan."
Ch. 18: "The bootstrap philosophy constitutes the final rejection of the mechanistic world view in modern physics." "...this view of nature came ever closer to the Eastern world view and is now in harmony with Eastern thought".
"The metaphor of Indra's net may justly be called the first bootstrap model, created by the Eastern sages some 2,500 years before the beginning of particle physics."
Ch. 16, 1st ed.: "At present, in the summer of 1974 ... No one has ever seen a quark, and according to our basic ideas about particle interactions quarks cannot exist." Footnote: "The recently discovered 'psi particles' may be of that kind."
End of ch. 18: "leading out of science and into the world of acintya, the unthinkable."
Epilogue: "I think all these questions have to be answered in the negative." (on whether physicists should meditate, or a synthesis is possible). "At present, our attitude is too yang ... too rational, male and aggressive."
1983 preface: "none of these recent developments has invalidated anything I wrote seven years ago."
1983 afterword: "most physicists still hang on to the idea of basic building blocks of matter". "Although our present understanding of the subatomic world precludes the existence of quarks as physical particles..." "not a single Nobel prize has so far been awarded to any of the outstanding physicists who contributed to the development of S-matrix theory".
1991 afterword: critics "would react with anger ... which reflected their own insecurity." "to the best of my knowledge nobody has found any factual errors in The Tao of Physics." "I knew with absolute certainty that the parallels ... would some day be common knowledge". On Chew: "the third revolutionary step in twentieth-century physics."
2019 (https://www.fritjofcapra.net/in-memoriam-geoffrey-chew/): "Today, bootstrap physics has virtually disappeared from the scene." "I made the close correspondence between Chew's bootstrap physics and Buddhist philosophy its high point and finale. I finished the book in London in December 1974".

## Rebukes
Bernstein, "A Cosmic Flow" (1978/79), in Science Observed (1982) pp. 333-340 (FTS): "What did Abraham Lincoln and Albert Einstein have in common?' Answer: 'They both had beards except for Einstein.'" "It is not that the Tao of Physics is wrong, but rather—in Pauli's phrase—it is not even wrong." "To hitch a religious philosophy to a contemporary science is a sure route to its obsolescence." Final lines: "What no one needs, in my opinion, is this superficial and profoundly misleading book."
Asimov, Skeptical Inquirer 5(2), 1980-81 (RAW): "if Taoist texts can only be properly understood after physicists reach the answers, then of what scientific use are the Taoist texts?" "And if modern physics changes its mind ... one person (still Capra, perhaps) will find other scraps of mystical tradition ... and come up with another match."
Lederman, The God Particle (1993) pp. 189-191 (FTS): "nothing wrong with finding parallels between the new quantum physics and Hinduism, Buddhism, Taoism, Zen, or Hunan cuisine, for that matter." "...a lunatic fringe of fakes, charlatans, and crazies. These folks guarantee eternal life if you restrict your diet to sumac roots."
Woit, Not Even Wrong (2006) pp. 141-145 (FTS): "in each of them Capra has left intact the now out-of-date physics, including new forewords and afterwords that with a straight face deny what has happened."
Wilber, Quantum Questions (1984) (FTS): "But nowadays virtually all physicists believe there are irreducible things (quarks, leptons, gluons) ... Does Buddha therefore lose his enlightenment?" "why bother with years of arduous meditation? Just read The Tao of Physics."
Stenger, The Unconscious Quantum (1995) (FTS): "bootstrap theory simply did not work." Quantum Quackery (1997, RAW): "'quantum' became a buzzword used to buttress the trendy, pseudoscientific spirituality".
Weinberg, Dreams of a Final Theory p. 77 (FTS): "I might, too, if I thought the opportunity was a real one, but I do not think it..."
Cox and Forshaw (2011) (FTS): "reams of drivel penned in its name."
Shermer, SciAm 2005 (RAW): "I challenge you to leap out of a 20-story building and consciously choose the experience of passing safely through the ground's tendencies."
Sokal hoax, note 27 (RAW), deadpan: "the book by Capra (1975) can be recommended for its scientific accuracy".
Ig Nobel 1998, Physics (RAW): "Deepak Chopra ... for his unique interpretation of quantum physics as it applies to life, liberty, and the pursuit of economic happiness."
David Albert on What the Bleep (Salon 2004, RAW): "I was edited in such a way as to completely suppress my actual views ... I am, indeed, profoundly unsympathetic to attempts at linking quantum mechanics with consciousness."
Dawkins/Chopra (unofficial transcript, check video): Chopra: "Oh it's just a metaphor"; physicists "have somehow hijacked the word".

## Physics dates
Gross, Nobel Lecture 2004 (RAW): "The bootstrap theory rested on two principles, both more philosophical than scientific." Nuclear democracy: "all were dynamical bound states of each other." Francis Low: "less of a theory than a tautology".
Asymptotic freedom 1973; J/psi "About November 10, 1974" (Nobel press release 1976); APS: the "November Revolution".
Book finished December 1974, published autumn 1975. In fairness: quark scepticism was respectable in summer 1974. The fair complaint is the 1983 and 1991 afterwords.
Sales (Kaiser 2011, FTS): first printing 20,000; Bantam 150,000 in 1977; half a million by 1983; "forty-three editions, including twenty-three translations ... millions of copies sold worldwide."

## Fairness, and the agent's own finding
The founders did remark on parallels, and Capra quotes them. But he cuts two of them just before their caveat.
Bohr (1937), next sentence after Capra's quote (RAW): "Still, the recognition of an analogy in the purely logical character of the problems ... does in no way imply acceptance in atomic physics of any mysticism foreign to the true spirit of science".
Oppenheimer (1954): the electron passage continues "The Buddha has given such answers ... but they are not familiar answers for the tradition of seventeenth- and eighteenth-century science."
No critic was seen making the truncation point. It is ours.
Capra is not Chopra: the epilogue denies that physicists should meditate and denies a synthesis. Lederman, Bernstein, Wilber and Shimony all concede he writes well and gets much physics right.
Defender: Victor Mansfield, Physics Today review (SECONDARY): "Capra, a high-energy theorist, has succeeded admirably."
