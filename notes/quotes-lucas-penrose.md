# Other people's rebukes of Lucas-Penrose and the microtubules (internal notes)

Collected 2026-09-19 by a research agent. Wording was checked against downloaded sources unless marked SECONDARY.

The Turing (1950) quote already on the site ("it has only been stated, without any sort of proof...") was checked against the paper: https://courses.cs.umbc.edu/471/papers/turing.pdf

1. Drew McDermott, "[STAR] Penrose is Wrong", PSYCHE 2(17), 1995. https://journalpsyche.org/files/0xaa29.pdf
   "But it's as if I presented a proof that all toasters were useless by hypothesizing a talking toaster and showing that it must burn at least one slice of toast."
   "Penrose stakes everything on his analysis of Gödel's Theorem. This analysis is all wrong, but what's striking is how much he tries to hang on it."
   "(One wonders where this leaves five-year-old children.)"
   "no one in his right mind would suppose that that human mathematicians "use" (or embody) a sound algorithm, let alone a "knowably" sound one. To verify this point, you need merely to find a case where a mathematician made a mistake."
   "Now that I've torn Penrose's argument to shreds, it's time for a spirited rebuttal of his critique of the computationalist theory of consciousness. Unfortunately, Penrose has no critique."
   "People like Penrose should declare victory and withdraw."

2. Patricia Churchland, "Brainshy" (1997/98), p. 14 of author's PDF. Primary order is pixie dust first.
   https://patriciachurchland.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/1997-Brainshy-NonNeural-Theories-of-Conscious-Experience.pdf
   "Pixie dust in the synapses is about as explanatorily powerful as quantum coherence in the microtubules. ... Penrose and Hameroff are offering a make-believe pig in a fantasy poke."
   "Is there any hard evidence that quantum coherence happens in microtubules? Only that it might. Surely the presence of cytoplasmic ions in the microtubule pore would disrupt these effects? They might not. [...] Somehow, it might."

3. Scott Aaronson, lecture 10.5 "Penrose". https://www.scottaaronson.com/democritus/lec10.5.html
   The Gödel CAPTCHA: "If you say you're a human, you pass. If, on the other hand, you say you're a machine, the program informs you that, while your answer happened to be correct in this instance, you clearly couldn't have arrived at it via a knowably sound procedure, since you don't possess the requisite microtubules. Therefore your request for an email account must unfortunately be denied."
   "consciousness is mysterious, quantum mechanics is also mysterious, therefore they must be related somehow!"
   ""Roger Penrose will never output this sentence.""
   Heading: "All Aboard the Holistic Quantum Gravy Train"
   Blog, 2016 (https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=2756): "about as eager to do that as to switch to a diet of microtubule casserole"; "a tiny subset of the population—a subset of mutants, freaks, and genetic oddballs. I … rest my case."

4. Dennett, Darwin's Dangerous Idea (1995), ch. 15, about p. 448.
   "(I can't resist raising one question for Penrose to ponder: if the magnificent quantum property lurks in the microtubules, does that mean that cockroaches have noncomputable minds, too? They have the same kind of microtubules we have.)"
   "...the only reason he has for thinking that a theory of quantum gravity would be noncomputable is that otherwise AI would be possible after all. In other words, Penrose has a hunch that someday we're going to find a skyhook."
   TLS review 1989, the chess syllogism: "(1) X is superbly capable of achieving checkmate. (2) There is no (practical) algorithm guaranteed to achieve checkmate. therefore (3) X does not owe its power to achieve checkmate to an algorithm."

5. Hans Moravec, "Roger Penrose's Gravitonic Brains", PSYCHE 2(6), 1995. https://journalpsyche.org/files/0xaa22.pdf
   Robot to future Penrose: "You did for machine intelligence in the twentieth century that Bishop Wilberforce did for Darwin's theory in the nineteenth."
   Penrose replies: "After defending the Gödel argument in 100 pages in my first book, I strengthened the defense to 200 pages in the second, 400 pages in the third, 800 pages in the fourth, and ... am in the process of preparing a 25,000 page rebuttal that should remove any remaining doubt."
   "Here's one: "Penrose must err to believe this sentence.""

6. John McCarthy, review of The Emperor's New Mind, Bull. AMS 23(2), 1990. http://www-formal.stanford.edu/jmc/reviews/penrose1/penrose1.html
   "Penrose: Tell me the logical system you use, and I'll tell you a true sentence you can't prove. Program: You tell me what system you use, and I'll tell you a true sentence you can't prove. Penrose: I don't use a fixed logical system. Program: I can use any system you like ... Would you like me to print you a manual? Your proposal is like a contest to see who can name the largest number with me going first."
   "The simplest reply to Penrose is that forming a Gödel sentence from a proof predicate expression is just a one line LISP program."
   "...the "strong AI" thesis which was invented by the philosopher John Searle in order to be refuted."

7. C. H. Whiteley (1962), via IEP: "Lucas cannot consistently assert this formula." https://iep.utm.edu/lp-argue/

8. Hilary Putnam, review of Shadows of the Mind (NYT 1994; Bull. AMS 32(3), 1995).
   "this reviewer regards its appearance as a sad episode in our current intellectual life."
   "He mistakenly believes that he has a philosophical disagreement with the logical community, when in fact this is a straightforward case of a mathematical fallacy."

9. Grush and Churchland, "Gaps in Penrose's Toilings", JCS 2(1), 1995.
   "merest possibility piled upon merest possibility teetering upon a tippy foundation of "might-be-for-all-we-know's"."

10. Minsky, "Conscious Machines" (1991): "the difficulty is not with making algorithms that can do reflection -- which is easy for machines, but with consistency -- which is hard for people."

11. Turing 1947 (via SEP): "if a machine is expected to be infallible, it cannot also be intelligent."
    Turing 1950: "There would be no question of triumphing simultaneously over all machines."
