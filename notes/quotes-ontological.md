# Ontological argument: quotations used on docs/ontological.html (internal notes)

Checked 2026-10-11 against texts fetched in this session (curl, then text extraction).
Wording below is as it appears on the page. Each entry gives the source fetched and the location.

## Anselm (defendant)
Source: Proslogium, trans. S. N. Deane (Open Court, 1903, repr. 1926), Fordham Internet Medieval Sourcebook,
https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/basis/anselm-proslogium.asp ("here modernized in some spellings").
- Ch. II: "And assuredly that, than which nothing greater can be conceived, cannot exist in the understanding alone. For, suppose it exists in the understanding alone: then it can be conceived to exist in reality; which is greater."
- Ch. III: "For, it is possible to conceive of a being which cannot be conceived not to exist; and this is greater than one which can be conceived not to exist."
- Caution: the Fordham text of ch. II has a typo, "Hence, there is doubt that there exists..." (should be "no doubt"). Not quoted.

Reply to Gaunilo, ch. III. Source: https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/basis/anselm-gaunilo.asp (same Deane edition).
- "Now I promise confidently that if any man shall devise anything existing either in reality or in concept alone (except that than which a greater be conceived) to which he can adapt the sequence of my reasoning, I will discover that thing, and will give him his lost island, not to be lost again."
  (The Fordham text drops a "cannot" in "than which a greater be conceived". Quoted as printed, with [cannot] supplied in brackets on the page.)

## Gaunilo
Source: In Behalf of the Fool, s.6, same Fordham page.
- "it is said that somewhere in the ocean is an island, which, because of the difficulty, or rather the impossibility, of discovering what does not exist, is called the lost island."
- "If a man should try to prove to me by such reasoning that this island truly exists, and that its existence should no longer be doubted, either I should believe that he was jesting, or I know not which I ought to regard as the greater fool: myself, supposing that I should allow this proof; or him, if he should suppose that he had established with any certainty the existence of this island."

## Descartes (successor)
Source: Meditations, trans. John Veitch (1853, repr. 1901), Wikisource, Meditation V, paras 8-10.
https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Meditations_on_First_Philosophy/Meditation_V (raw wikitext fetched)
- Para 8: "it is not less impossible to conceive a God, that is, a being supremely perfect, to whom existence is awanting, or who is devoid of a certain perfection, than to conceive a mountain without a valley."
- Para 9: "for my thought imposes no necessity on things; and as I may imagine a winged horse, though there be none such, so I could perhaps attribute existence to God, though no God existed."
- Para 10: "because I cannot conceive God unless as existing, it follows that existence is inseparable from him, and therefore that he really exists"

## Plantinga (successor)
Source: The Nature of Necessity (Oxford: Clarendon, 1974), ch. X. Text checked against the archive.org scan
https://archive.org/details/alvin-plantinga-the-nature-of-necessity_202410 (djvu text, an Oxford Scholarship Online

- p.219: "Logic tells us that A and D cannot both be sound; but it also tells us they cannot both be unsound; one is sound and the other is not."
- p.219: "It must be conceded, however, that Argument A is not a successful piece of natural theology."
- p.220: "a sane and rational man who thought it through and understood it might none the less reject it, remaining agnostic or even accepting instead the possibility of no-maximality."

- p.221: "They cannot, perhaps, be said to prove or establish their conclusion. But since it is rational to accept their central premiss, they do show that it is rational to accept that conclusion. And perhaps that is all that can be expected of any such argument."

## Kant
Source: Critique of Pure Reason, trans. J. M. D. Meiklejohn, Gutenberg #4280, Transcendental Dialectic, Bk II ch. III s.4
("Of the Impossibility of an Ontological Proof of the Existence of God"). https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4280/pg4280.txt
- "Being is evidently not a real predicate, that is, a conception of something which is added to the conception of some other thing."
- "A hundred real dollars contain no more than a hundred possible dollars."
- "to suppose the non-existence of both triangle and angles is perfectly admissible."
- "we may as well hope to increase our stock of knowledge by the aid of mere ideas, as the merchant to augment his wealth by the addition of noughts to his cash account."
- Trap observed: "existence is not a predicate" is a slogan, not Kant's sentence; Meiklejohn says "dollars".

## Hume
Source: Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion, Part IX, Gutenberg #4583. https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4583/pg4583.txt
- Speaker is CLEANTHES, not Philo; the page says so.
- "Whatever we conceive as existent, we can also conceive as non-existent. There is no being, therefore, whose non-existence implies a contradiction."
- Caution: Part IX answers Demea's a priori (cosmological, Clarke-style) argument, not Anselm by name. Page says it is aimed at all a priori proofs of existence.

## SEP
Source: Oppy, G., Rasmussen, J., and Schmid, J., "Ontological Arguments", Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy
(substantive revision 3 June 2024). https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ontological-arguments/
- s.8 (modal ontological arguments): "the MOA is dialectically toothless—quite clearly, if you don't already accept the claim that God exists, you won't agree that (i) is more acceptable than (i*) absent some symmetry breaker."
- s.9 (Gödel): "Unless we have an independent, non-question-begging way to discern which properties are positive, this looks like game over for Gödelian ontological arguments."
- s.13: "under what conditions can one reasonably believe something of the form, 'it is possible that F' without merely deriving that conclusion from 'it is actual that F'?"
- s.13: "it is not plausible that 'God exists and 2+2=4 therefore God exists' is a successful argument, even though all monotheists accept that it is (classically) sound."
- s.13: "It may well be that theists have—or can have—good reasons for thinking that there are sound ontological arguments."
- ATTRIBUTION TRAP: the 2024 entry is jointly authored (Oppy, Rasmussen, Schmid). "Dialectically toothless" should not be credited to Oppy alone, as the earlier notes did.

## The formal rebuke
The derivation is standard (SEP s.8 gives the "Reverse MOA"). Checked by hand: in S5 the accessibility relation is
universal, so the necessity premise box(G -> box G) holds iff G is true in all worlds or in none. Then diamond G <-> G
and diamond not-G <-> not-G. Credited to SEP and to Plantinga's own Argument D.
