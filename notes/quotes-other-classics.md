# Ontological argument, Pascal, Doomsday/simulation, Zeno, and scientists on philosophy (internal notes)

Collected 2026-09-19 by a research agent. Wording was checked against downloaded sources unless marked.
Some sources are OCR or transcriptions; check punctuation against print before publishing.

## Attribution traps
1. "Philosophy of science is as useful to scientists as ornithology is to birds" is NOT Feynman. Quote Investigator: "QI has found no substantive support for the attribution to Richard Feynman." Weinberg repeated it as hearsay in Nature (1987); ancestor is the painter Barnett Newman (1955). https://quoteinvestigator.com/2023/04/09/science-birds/
2. Russell's exclamation exists in two genuine versions: "Great God in boots" (My Mental Development, 1944) and "Great Scott" (Autobiography, 1967). Cite whichever work is named.
3. Kant: "existence is not a predicate" is a slogan, not his sentence. Meiklejohn's translation says "dollars", not "thalers".
4. "Solvitur ambulando" is a later tag. Diogenes Laertius does not name Zeno; the link comes from Simplicius.
5. Gasking's parody was reconstructed by William Grey (Analysis 60(4), 2000).
6. Hossenfelder's "pseudoscience" is a post title. Her sharper line: "It's not even pseudoscience."
7. Cicero's popular wording ("nothing so absurd that some philosopher has not said it") is a free rendering, not the Loeb text.
8. Hawking's Wittgenstein "quote" appears in no Wittgenstein text.
9. Bierce's Zeno joke is about Zeno of Citium, the wrong Zeno.

## Ontological argument
- Schopenhauer, Fourfold Root s.7: "Considered by daylight, however, and without prejudice, this famous Ontological Proof is really a charming joke." Also "the sleight-of-hand trick". https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/50966/pg50966-images.html
- Dawkins, The God Delusion ch. 3: "Let me translate this infantile argument into the appropriate language, which is the language of the playground: 'Bet you I can prove God exists.' 'Bet you can't.' 'Right then, imagine the most perfect perfect perfect thing possible.' ... 'So I've proved that God exists. Nur Nurny Nur Nur. All atheists are fools.'"
  "I once piqued a gathering of theologians and philosophers by adapting the ontological argument to prove that pigs can fly. They felt the need to resort to Modal Logic to prove that I was wrong."
  On Gasking: "The only difference is, Gasking was being funny on purpose."
- Gasking's proof (via Grey, as quoted by Dawkins): "... 4. The most formidable handicap for a creator would be non-existence. ... Ergo: 7. God does not exist."
- Gaunilo, In Behalf of the Fool s.6: "either I should believe that he was jesting, or I know not which I ought to regard as the greater fool: myself, supposing that I should allow this proof; or him..." https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/basis/anselm-gaunilo.asp
- Kant (Meiklejohn): "we may as well hope to increase our stock of knowledge by the aid of mere ideas, as the merchant to augment his wealth by the addition of noughts to his cash account." https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4280/pg4280.txt
- Russell, History of Western Philosophy: "it is easier to feel convinced that it must be fallacious than it is to find out precisely where the fallacy lies." And: "Every philosopher would like to say yes, because a philosopher's job is to find out things about the world by thinking rather than observing."
- Russell, My Mental Development: "I suddenly threw it up in the air and exclaimed as I caught it: 'Great God in boots, the ontological argument is sound.'"
- Hume, Dialogues IX: "there is an evident absurdity in pretending to demonstrate a matter of fact, or to prove it by any arguments a priori."
- Oppy (SEP): Plantinga's modal version is "dialectically toothless". https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ontological-arguments/

## Pascal's Wager
- Homer Simpson, "Homer the Heretic" (1992): "And what if we picked the wrong religion? Every week, we're just making God madder and madder!" (fan transcript) https://www.simpsonsarchive.com/episodes/9F01.html
- William James, The Will to Believe s.II: "if we were ourselves in the place of the Deity, we should probably take particular pleasure in cutting off believers of this pattern from their infinite reward." https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/26659/pg26659.txt
- Hitchens, God Is Not Great ch. 15: "His celebrated "wager" puts it in hucksterish form: what have you got to lose?" Also "this cunning piece of bet-covering".
- Dawkins: "Pascal's Wager could only ever be an argument for feigning belief in God. And the God that you claim to believe in had better not be of the omniscient kind or he'd see through the deception."
- Hájek (SEP s.5), mixed strategies: "Suppose that you choose to ignore the Wager, and to go and have a hamburger instead. Still, you may well assign positive and finite probability to your winding up wagering for God nonetheless; and this probability multiplied by infinity again gives infinity." https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pascal-wager/
- Diderot (SEP translation): "An Imam could reason just as well this way".
- Voltaire (via SEP): "That article seems a bit indecent and childish; that notion of gambling, of losses and winnings, does not suit the gravity of the subject".
- Bostrom, "Pascal's mugging", Analysis 69(3), 2009: "Mugger: Hey, give me your wallet. Pascal: Why on Earth would I want to do that? Mugger: Otherwise I'll shoot you. Pascal: But you don't have a gun. Mugger: Oops! I knew I had forgotten something." Ends: "Pascal hands over his wallet." https://nickbostrom.com/papers/pascal.pdf

## Doomsday and simulation
- Hossenfelder (2017): ""The programmer did it" isn't science. It's not even pseudoscience. It's just words." And: "Most readers will instead correctly conclude that today's intelligencia is full of shit. And I can't even blame them for it." http://backreaction.blogspot.com/2017/03/no-we-probably-dont-live-in-computer.html
- Carlton Caves on Gott (arXiv:0806.3538): "To sweeten the pot, I offered Gott 2:1 odds in his favor. Gott refused the bets on the grounds that "I don't do bets."" Later: "I contacted the caretakers during May and June of 2008 and verified that all six dogs have died. Thus, as I fully expected, I would have won all the bets and been $6,000 US richer."
- Caves (astro-ph/0001414): "he rejects as irrelevant the process of rational, scientific inquiry, replacing it with a single, universal statistical rule. That has to be wrong."
- Andrew Gelman (2007): "The doomsday argument is pretty silly (and also, it's not Bayesian)." (2018): "Hey, living to the age of 270—that'd be cool! I just have to make it to 90, then I'm most of the way there."
- Aaronson: "the simulation hypothesis is lazy—that it doesn't pay its rent by doing real explanatory work". https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=3208
- Lisa Randall (reported, Sci Am 2016): "I actually am very interested in why so many people think it's an interesting question."
- Sean Carroll (2016): "that's a fake kind of humility — by asserting that "we are typical" in the multiverse, we're actually claiming that "typical observers are like us.""

## Zeno
- Diogenes Laertius VI.39 (Hicks): "when somebody declared that there is no such thing as motion, he got up and walked about." https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/Book_VI
- Feynman Lectures I s.8-2: "The Greeks were somewhat confused by such problems, being helped, of course, by some very confusing Greeks." (transcription; check Caltech page)
- Peirce, as quoted by Salmon: "this ridiculous little catch presents no difficulty at all to a mind adequately trained in mathematics and logic."
- Stoppard, Jumpers (UNVERIFIED, Wikiquote only): "...and Saint Sebastian died of fright."
- Whitehead (secondary): "No one has ever touched Zeno without refuting him, and every century thinks it worthwhile to refute him"
- Russell, Principles of Mathematics s.327, defends Zeno: "the grossness of subsequent philosophers pronounced him to be a mere ingenious juggler". Zeno may deserve an acquittal.
- Aristotle, Physics VI.9: "time is not composed of indivisible moments".

## Scientists and mathematicians on philosophy
- Krauss (The Atlantic, 2012): "science progresses and philosophy doesn't." And "the only people, as far as I can tell, that read work by philosophers of science are other philosophers of science."
- Dyson (NYRB, 2012): "Compared with the giants of the past, they are a sorry bunch of dwarfs." https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2012/11/08/what-can-you-really-know/
- Feynman Lectures I ch. 2: "Philosophers, incidentally, say a great deal about what is absolutely necessary for science, and it is always, so far as one can see, rather naive, and probably wrong."
- Feynman I s.8-1 (low-grade source, check): "that paralysis of thought that comes to philosophers, who sit opposite each other, one saying to the other "You don't know what you are talking about!" The second one says "What do you mean by know? What do you mean by talking? What do you mean by you?""
- Weinberg, Dreams of a Final Theory ch. 7: "The insights of philosophers have occasionally benefited physicists, but generally in a negative fashion—by protecting them from the preconceptions of other philosophers." Also "the unreasonable ineffectiveness of philosophy." And on jargon: "aimed at impressing those who confound obscurity with profundity."
- Cicero, De Divinatione II.58 (Falconer): "Somehow or other no statement is too absurd for some philosophers to make." https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cicero/de_Divinatione/2*.html
- Hume, Enquiry XII: "Commit it then to the flames: for it can contain nothing but sophistry and illusion." (target is divinity and school metaphysics)
